Abstract

Hyperspectral cameras see beyond RGB — they resolve a scene's reflectance across dozens of narrow spectral bands, revealing material properties invisible to conventional imaging. The catch: more spectral channels means less light per channel, forcing a tradeoff between spectral resolution, exposure time, and frame rate that no existing system fully resolves.

Lumosaic escapes this tradeoff through joint hardware co-design: a programmable narrowband LED array and a coded-exposure-pixel (CEP) sensor work in lockstep, encoding spectral information into both illumination and pixel-level exposure simultaneously — rather than relying on passive optics that attenuate light and assume a static scene. This produces a 31-channel hyperspectral video at 30 fps and native VGA resolution, with demonstrated applications in real-time metamerism disambiguation and spectrally resolved capture of dynamic phenomena such as diffusion and effervescence.

Method

⚙️ Pipeline Overview Figure Diagram: LED cycling → CEP exposure schedule → spatio-spectro-temporal mosaic → learning-based reconstruction network

Overview of the Lumosaic sensing and reconstruction pipeline.

Active Illumination

A programmable array of 12 narrowband LEDs cycles through wavelengths from 400–700 nm at microsecond precision — delivering structured spectral illumination rather than filtering broadband light that attenuates photon count.

Coded-Exposure Pixels

Each pixel integrates light according to a spatially tiled, per-frame exposure schedule synchronized with the LED cycle — embedding spectral identity directly into the sensor readout without any dispersive optics.

Together, these two components produce a dense spatio-spectro-temporal mosaic within every captured frame. A learning-based reconstruction pipeline then performs temporal alignment of spectral sub-images and recovers the full hyperspectral cube per frame using a deep network trained on the system's forward model.
